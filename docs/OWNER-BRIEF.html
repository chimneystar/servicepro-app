Software Review

ServicePro: what we found, and what we’re going to do about it

We completed a full review of the system — every screen, every payment path, and the database underneath. The foundation is genuinely good. A number of specific faults are serious, and we are fixing all of them before this serves a single customer.

Prepared 31 July 2026 · Full technical detail available on request

The short versionThe system is worth keeping — and it is not ready yet

We considered rebuilding from scratch and decided against it, deliberately. The hardest parts to get right — keeping each business’s data separate, calculating money to the cent, securing card details, and the full Hebrew and English interface — are already done correctly. Rebuilding would cost roughly twice as much and would put those at risk.

What is wrong is concentrated in the newer, faster-built parts of the system: gaps in who is allowed to do what, a handful of billing errors, and missing safety nets. Those are all fixable, and we are fixing everything — not only the urgent items.

What we found — 01Faults that could affect your customers

Deposits are not credited to the final invoiceUrgent

A customer who pays a deposit on a quote is then billed the full amount again. Nothing in the system links the deposit to the invoice, and no screen would reveal the mistake.

Signed documents can be re-signed by anyone with the linkUrgent

An approved quote can have its signature replaced with a different name at any time. If a job were ever disputed, the original signature would not stand up.

Customer portal links never expire and cannot be cancelledUrgent

One forwarded email gives permanent access to that customer’s full history — and to the payment links on every one of their invoices.

Text messages can be faked into your inboxHigh

The system does not verify that an incoming text genuinely came from the phone network, and can file a message under the wrong business entirely.

What we found — 02Faults in your numbers

Invoices count money you have not receivedHigh

Declined cards and bank transfers still in progress are shown as paid, so an invoice can look settled when it is not.

Revenue and profit figures are overstatedHigh

The dashboard counts invoice totals rather than money actually collected, and the profit figure leaves out discounts and tax. These are the numbers used for payroll and tax decisions.

Commission is paid on quoted work, not collected workHigh

Technicians earn commission on jobs the business was never paid for.

Repeat-maintenance jobs clog the dispatch boardHigh

Jobs created automatically each night reappear on every future day and never clear. The board degrades on its own, with no one doing anything wrong.

What we found — 03Who can do what

Any staff member can promote themselves to ownerUrgent

There are two separate ways a technician or office user can give themselves full owner rights — including access to payment settings and financial records. This is the single most important thing we found.

Technicians can change their own commission rate and colleagues’ timesheetsHigh

They can also see every colleague’s location history, supplier costs and marketing spend.

Anyone signed in can send an email as your businessHigh

Branded with your name and logo, to any address, containing any link.

What we found — 04What happens when something goes wrong

A nightly task deletes customer records and is not password-protectedUrgent

Because of how it was configured, it can currently be triggered by anyone on the internet.

The written recovery instructions rebuild the wrong systemUrgent

They stop partway through, so following them after a failure produces a database the software cannot run on. There is also no backup or rollback procedure written down at all.

Nothing tells anyone when the system failsHigh

There is no alerting. The nightly job reports success even when every part of it has failed, so a problem could run for weeks unnoticed.

The planWe fix everything, in this order

Security comes first because it decides whether the system can go live at all. Nothing already working will be removed — we catalogued all ~190 existing capabilities first, specifically so that nothing is lost along the way. Every screen in the system is treated as needed, and the unfinished ones get finished rather than deleted.

What we’re addingCapabilities the system is missing

The review also asked a second question: what should be here that is not? These are the gaps worth closing — some because the business is exposed without them, some because they are simply what a service company needs.

Protecting your records

Credit notes and invoice cancellationNew

Today there is no correct way to cancel or correct an invoice you have already issued — only editing it, which rewrites history. Accountants and tax authorities require better.

A full record of every change to moneyNew

Jobs, invoices and quotes keep a change history. Payments do not — the one table where it matters most.

Guaranteed invoice numbering, plus undoNew

Nothing currently prevents two invoices sharing a number, and deleted records cannot be recovered from any screen.

Locking documents once sent or paidNew

So the figures a customer approved cannot quietly change afterwards.

A complete export of your own business dataNew

Your data should be yours to take, in full, at any time.

Trust and accountability

Proper evidence on signatures and loginsNew

The system records nothing about where an action came from. A signed quote captures only a name and a drawing — thin evidence in a dispute.

Two-step login, and the ability to sign out a lost phoneNew

An owner account controls payouts and customer data behind a single password today.

An activity log you can actually readNew

“What changed in my business last week, and who did it?” is not answerable today. Changes to staff permissions leave no trace at all.

Protection against repeated password guessing, and STOP handling for textsNew

A customer replying STOP is currently stored as an ordinary message and reminders keep sending — a legal risk in several markets.

Running the business better

Parts used on a job come out of stock automaticallyNew

Stock is a standalone counter today; nothing decrements it when a technician fits a part, so it drifts out of date within a week — and job costs exclude materials.

True job profitability, including labourNew

Clock-in data is collected but never reaches any profit figure.

Technician holiday and time offNew

Booking and dispatch will currently schedule someone who is away.

Good / better / best quote optionsNew

The single biggest lever on quote acceptance, and the system can only produce one flat price.

Notifications for your staffNew

Nothing currently tells a technician they have been assigned a job, or tells you that a payment arrived.

Customer statements, appointment confirmations and calendar syncNew

Plus a weekly summary by email, and the ability to act on many jobs or invoices at once instead of one at a time.

In closingWhat this means for you

Nothing you can see today is being taken away. The work is sequenced so that the system becomes safe first, correct second, and richer third — and every fix ships with a test that proves it stayed fixed, which is the part that was missing until now.

Two questions have now been settled, and both shape the plan above. Every screen stays. The roughly one third of the system that is unfinished groundwork will be completed rather than removed — so features that today look real but do nothing, such as refunds, tips, automatic follow-up campaigns and stock movements, will actually work. And invoicing stays in US dollars, which matches the card provider already integrated; we will remove the other currency options from setup so no one can select one that payments cannot handle.

The commitment is straightforward: we work through the list until there is nothing left on it. Progress is tracked in the project itself rather than in status emails, so at any point you can ask what is done, what is in flight, and what remains — and get an answer grounded in the software, not in a summary.
